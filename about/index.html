About PDFMango

PDFMango is a free PDF tool that runs entirely in your browser. It merges PDFs, puts pages in the right order, turns pages that are the wrong way round, deletes the ones you don't need, turns JPG and PNG photos, Word documents and text files into PDF pages, saves pages as pictures, and makes big files small enough to email. Your files never leave your device: there is no upload, no account and nothing stored.

Open PDFMango

Why PDFMango

I often need to do simple things with PDFs: put the pages in the right order, turn a page that is the wrong way round, join two documents, or make a large file small enough to send by email. Every time I looked for a tool for this, I ended up with something full of ads, cluttered with features I did not need, wanting me to upload my documents to someone else's server, or working on only one platform. I could not find one that I felt was reliable, safe, modern and simple, all at the same time.

So I decided to have one built. PDFMango brings together well-established open-source PDF libraries, and I used Claude AI to design and develop it. Your files never leave your device; all the work happens inside your browser. There are no ads, no sign-ups and no clutter, and it works the same way on Windows, Mac, Linux, Android and iPhone.

PDFMango is completely open source. The full source code is on GitHub, free for anyone to use, learn from, improve and benefit from.

— Venkatarangan Thirumalai

How to use

  1. Drop PDFs, JPG/PNG photos, Word (.docx) or text (.txt) files onto the page, or press Choose files.
  2. Drag pages into the order you want (on a phone, press and hold a page, then drag).
  3. Select pages to rotate or delete them; with nothing selected, the toolbar acts on every page. Page tools (on a phone, More) duplicates pages, inserts a blank page, reverses the order and selects odd or even pages.
  4. Press Save all pages and pick a compression level: Lossless keeps everything, Balanced and Strong shrink photos, Scan makes the smallest file.
  5. Press Preview & Share to check the result, then Download it or Share it to another app. Direct Download skips the check.
  6. To save pages as pictures, choose Images at the top of the same window. Phones can share up to 10 images at a time; on a computer they download as one ZIP.
  7. To save only some pages, select them: a bar appears with Extract as PDF and Extract as images. The main button always saves every page.

Word and text files

Word (.docx) and text (.txt) files are turned into A4 pages on your device, so you can merge them with PDFs and photos. Headings, bold and italic, lists, tables and pictures come across; Word's exact fonts, headers, footers, page numbers and text boxes do not, and page breaks can fall in different places. Text stays selectable, though in Tamil and Hindi a vowel sign written before its letter (as in கொ or कि) may copy out of order. For an exact copy, save the file as PDF from Word.

Besides English, they can be in Chinese, Japanese and Korean, the main Indian languages (Tamil, Hindi, Marathi, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam, Bengali, Gujarati, Punjabi and Odia) and Arabic or Urdu. Other languages aren't supported yet: to keep downloads small, PDFMango fetches a language's font only the first time a file needs it. Older Word files (.doc) need to be saved as .docx first.

Privacy and analytics

PDFMango uses Google Analytics to count anonymous visits to the live site. Nothing about your files (their names, contents, pages or sizes) is ever collected or sent anywhere; your PDFs, images, Word and text files are processed entirely inside your browser. Analytics runs only on the live site (never while testing locally), waits until the app has fully loaded, and skips itself automatically if you are offline or your browser sends a Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control signal. Google sets its own first-party cookies to measure traffic; see Google's privacy policy for details. PDFMango's full source is open on GitHub.

PDFMango sets no cookies. If you change its Settings, only those choices are saved in your browser (Reset removes them); nothing about your files is ever stored. To work offline, your browser keeps a copy of the app's own code and any language fonts it has fetched, never your files. When you turn a phone photo into a page, PDFMango also removes the photo's hidden details, such as its GPS location, without touching the picture itself.

Open source

The complete source code is on GitHub at github.com/venkatarangan/pdfmango, under the GNU Affero General Public License v3.0.

PDFMango is built on:

  • MuPDF.js by Artifex Software — the PDF engine (AGPL-3.0)
  • mammoth.js — reading Word files (BSD-2-Clause)
  • Noto fonts — Indian scripts and Arabic (SIL Open Font License)
  • Svelte and Vite (MIT)
  • SortableJS — drag and drop (MIT)
  • Comlink — talking to the background worker (Apache-2.0)
  • vite-plugin-pwa and Workbox — offline support (MIT)
  • Material Symbols — icons (Apache-2.0)

Built with AI

PDFMango was generated with Claude Opus 5.5, Anthropic's AI model, working in Claude Code. Venkatarangan Thirumalai wrote the specification and made the product decisions; Claude designed and wrote the code, the tests and this documentation, tested the PDF engine before building on it, and checked its work with automated tests on desktop and phone layouts.

Everything is open: the original plan, the prompts used to build it and the engine spike findings are in the source repository, so anyone can see exactly how it was made.
